## Real-life scenario references

### CPAQA_005 — Airline refunds for lockdown cancellations

- **Case name:** Pravasi Legal Cell & Ors. v. Union of India & Ors.
- **Case citation:** Writ Petition (Civil) (Diary No. 10966/2020) with connected matters; reported [2020] 12 SCR 102 (secondary sources differ on the case number)
- **Court/commission:** Supreme Court of India (3-judge bench: Ashok Bhushan, R. Subhash Reddy, M.R. Shah, JJ.)
- **Decision date:** 1 October 2020
- **Source URL:** https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2020/10966/10966_2020_35_1501_24201_Judgement_01-Oct-2020.pdf — *opened (HTTP 200)*
- **Additional source:** https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2020/10/02/booked-air-tickets-during-lockdown-supreme-court-orders-full-refund-read-directions/ — *opened (HTTP 200)*
- **Source type:** Official judgment (Supreme Court website); secondary report
- **Applicable legislation:** Writ jurisdiction (Article 32); not a Consumer Protection Act proceeding
- **Material facts:** Passengers' flights were cancelled during the COVID-19 lockdown and airlines issued credit shells instead of refunds.
- **Legal issue:** Whether airlines must refund fares for flights cancelled during the lockdown.
- **Actual outcome:** Full refund directed for tickets booked during the lockdown for travel within the lockdown period; for other cases a transferable credit shell was permitted, with unused credit to be refunded by 31 March 2021.
- **Reasoning:** Accepted the framework proposed by the Ministry/DGCA as balancing passenger rights and airline viability.
- **Relevant provisions:** Not decided under the CPA; dataset maps the scenario to CPA 2019 ss. 2(42), 2(11), 2(47), 39.
- **Verification status:** Verified against secondary reports only.
- **Limitations:** Writ proceeding under exceptional circumstances; outcome is not a consumer-commission ruling and should not be generalised to ordinary cancellations.
- **Dataset reference_sections:** CPA_2_42, CPA_2_11, CPA_2_47, CPA_39

### CPAQA_009 — Ola coupon-only refunds and missing auto-ride receipts

- **Case name:** Central Consumer Protection Authority order/direction concerning ANI Technologies Pvt. Ltd. (Ola)
- **Case citation:** CCPA order (reference number not located)
- **Court/commission:** Central Consumer Protection Authority (regulator under CPA 2019, s. 10)
- **Decision date:** Reported around 13 October 2024
- **Source URL:** https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/ccpa-orders-ola-to-offer-refund-choices-provide-auto-ride-receipts — *opened (HTTP 200)*
- **Additional source:** https://newsonair.gov.in/ccpa-directs-ola-to-develop-mechanism-providing-choice-to-consumers-regarding-refund-mode — *opened (HTTP 200)*
- **Source type:** Secondary case report (press coverage; All India Radio news)
- **Applicable legislation:** CPA 2019
- **Material facts:** Ola's refund policy offered only coupons for future rides, and auto-ride invoices/receipts were not issued; the CCPA reportedly logged 2,061 complaints against Ola from January to October 2024.
- **Legal issue:** Whether coupon-only refunds and non-issue of receipts are unfair trade practices.
- **Actual outcome:** CCPA directed Ola to let consumers choose a bank-account refund or a coupon, and to issue invoices/receipts for auto rides.
- **Reasoning:** Non-issue of bills and restricted refund choice infringe consumer rights (press summary).
- **Relevant provisions:** CPA 2019 ss. 2(47)(vii), 17, 18, 20
- **Verification status:** Verified against secondary reports only.
- **Limitations:** Verified against press reports of the CCPA order; the order text itself was not reviewed. The exact statutory section relied on by the CCPA was not confirmed.
- **Dataset reference_sections:** CPA_2_47, CPA_17, CPA_18, CPA_20

### CPAQA_011 — Courier liability cap in a signed consignment note

- **Case name:** Bharathi Knitting Company v. DHL Worldwide Express Courier Division of Airfreight Ltd.
- **Case citation:** (1996) 4 SCC 704; AIR 1996 SC 2508; Civil Appeal No. 9057 of 1996
- **Court/commission:** Supreme Court of India
- **Decision date:** 9 May 1996
- **Source URL:** https://indiankanoon.org/doc/126678/ — *opened (HTTP 200)*
- **Additional source:** https://www.legalauthority.in/judgement/bharathi-knitting-company-vs-dhl-worldwide-express-courier-division-of-airfreight-ltd-25983 — *opened (HTTP 200)*
- **Source type:** Reliable judgment repository (Indian Kanoon); secondary summaries
- **Applicable legislation:** CPA 1986
- **Material facts:** The appellant sent documents abroad through DHL; the signed consignment note capped DHL's liability at US$100; the consignment was lost.
- **Legal issue:** Whether the consumer forum could award more than the contractual liability limit.
- **Actual outcome:** Appeal dismissed; NCDRC's award limited to US$100 upheld.
- **Reasoning:** The sender signed the consignment note and was bound by its terms.
- **Relevant provisions:** CPA 1986 (deficiency in service); dataset maps to CPA 2019 ss. 2(42), 2(11), 2(46), 49, 2(7).
- **Verification status:** Verified against secondary reports only.
- **Limitations:** Verified against secondary reports (legal news / case summaries) and official-record search excerpts; official URL located but full judgment text not reviewed in this pass. Later NCDRC decisions (e.g. Blue Dart v. Stephen Livera) distinguished it for unsigned fine print; CPA 2019's 'unfair contract' concept post-dates the decision.
- **Dataset reference_sections:** CPA_2_42, CPA_2_11, CPA_2_46, CPA_49, CPA_2_7

### CPAQA_014 — 20% earnest-money forfeiture held excessive

- **Case name:** Godrej Projects Development Limited v. Anil Karlekar & Ors.
- **Case citation:** 2025 INSC 143; Civil Appeal No. 3334 of 2023
- **Court/commission:** Supreme Court of India (B.R. Gavai and S.V. Bhatti, JJ.)
- **Decision date:** 3 February 2025
- **Source URL:** https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2023/1650/1650_2023_2_1501_59163_Judgement_03-Feb-2025.pdf — *opened (HTTP 200)*
- **Additional source:** https://www.verdictum.in/court-updates/supreme-court/godrej-projects-development-limited-v-anil-karlekar-2025-insc-143-earnest-money-forfeiture-1566876 — *opened (HTTP 200)*
- **Source type:** Official judgment (Supreme Court website); secondary report
- **Applicable legislation:** Consumer protection law (complaint before NCDRC); Indian Contract Act s. 74 discussed
- **Material facts:** Buyers of a Gurugram flat cancelled; the agreement allowed forfeiture of 20% of the Basic Sale Price as earnest money.
- **Legal issue:** Whether forfeiture of 20% of BSP was valid.
- **Actual outcome:** Appeal partly allowed. Reasonable forfeiture of earnest money is not a penalty, but the agreement was one-sided and 20% forfeiture was excessive and arbitrary.
- **Reasoning:** Relied on Maula Bux and on Pioneer/Ireo line on one-sided clauses.
- **Relevant provisions:** Dataset maps to CPA 2019 ss. 2(46), 2(47), 49, 59, 39.
- **Verification status:** Verified against secondary reports only.
- **Limitations:** Verified against secondary reports (legal news / case summaries) and official-record search excerpts; official URL located but full judgment text not reviewed in this pass. - **Dataset reference_sections:** CPA_2_46, CPA_2_47, CPA_49, CPA_59, CPA_39

### CPAQA_022 — Warranty covers replacement of defective part, not the car

- **Case name:** Maruti Udyog Ltd. v. Susheel Kumar Gabgotra & Anr.
- **Case citation:** (2006) 4 SCC 644; AIR 2006 SC 1586
- **Court/commission:** Supreme Court of India (Arijit Pasayat and Tarun Chatterjee, JJ.)
- **Decision date:** 29 March 2006
- **Source URL:** https://indiankanoon.org/doc/192244/ — *opened (HTTP 200)*
- **Additional source:** https://digiscr.sci.gov.in/admin/judgement_file/judgement_pdf/2006/volume%203/Part%20I/2006_3_603-609_1702098239.pdf — *could not connect from the checking machine (DNS/network)*
- **Source type:** Reliable judgment repository; official SCR PDF
- **Applicable legislation:** CPA 1986 context (matter reached SC from the High Court)
- **Material facts:** A car bought in November 1996 had a recurring defect during warranty; the warranty obliged Maruti only to repair or replace defective parts; the High Court directed replacement of the vehicle.
- **Legal issue:** Whether the warranty required replacement of the whole car.
- **Actual outcome:** Appeal allowed in part: the High Court was not justified in directing replacement of the vehicle; the defective part was to be replaced free and compensation paid.
- **Reasoning:** The warranty clause clearly referred to replacement of the defective part.
- **Relevant provisions:** Dataset maps to CPA 2019 ss. 2(10), 2(20), 39, 84.
- **Verification status:** Verified against secondary reports only.
- **Limitations:** Verified against secondary reports (legal news / case summaries) and official-record search excerpts; official URL located but full judgment text not reviewed in this pass. - **Dataset reference_sections:** CPA_2_10, CPA_2_20, CPA_39, CPA_84

### CPAQA_023 — Airbags failed to deploy

- **Case name:** Hyundai Motor India Limited v. Shailendra Bhatnagar
- **Case citation:** Civil Appeal No. 3001 of 2022; 2022 LiveLaw (SC) 399
- **Court/commission:** Supreme Court of India (Vineet Saran and Aniruddha Bose, JJ.)
- **Decision date:** 20 April 2022
- **Source URL:** https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2021/5691/5691_2021_9_1502_35207_Judgement_20-Apr-2022.pdf — *opened (HTTP 200)*

- **Source type:** Official judgment (Supreme Court website); secondary report
- **Applicable legislation:** CPA 1986 (complaint filed before the 2019 Act)
- **Material facts:** A Creta bought in 2015 with two front airbags crashed in November 2017; the airbags did not deploy and the driver suffered head, chest and dental injuries. The State Commission ordered replacement and compensation; NCDRC upheld it.

### CPAQA_029 — Dealer sold old car as new; manufacturer not liable without proof of knowledge

- **Case name:** Tata Motors Ltd. v. Antonio Paulo Vaz & Anr.
- **Case citation:** Civil Appeal No. 574 of 2021; LL 2021 SC 105
- **Court/commission:** Supreme Court of India (U.U. Lalit, Hemant Gupta, S. Ravindra Bhat, JJ.)
- **Decision date:** 18 February 2021
- **Source URL:** https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2020/13758/13758_2020_38_1503_26259_Judgement_18-Feb-2021.pdf — *opened (HTTP 200)*
- **Additional source:** https://barandbench.com/news/litigation/sale-defective-car-manufacturer-not-liable-unless-knowledge-proved-supreme-court — *opened (HTTP 200)*
- **Source type:** Official judgment (Supreme Court website); secondary report
- **Applicable legislation:** CPA 1986
- **Material facts:** Buyer paid in 2011 for a new car; the dealer delivered a 2009-model car that had run 622 km. Forums held dealer and manufacturer jointly liable.
- **Legal issue:** Whether the manufacturer is liable for the dealer's conduct.
- **Actual outcome:** Manufacturer's appeal allowed; liability against Tata Motors set aside.
- **Reasoning:** Relationship was principal-to-principal; manufacturer's knowledge was neither pleaded nor proved.
- **Relevant provisions:** Dataset maps to CPA 2019 ss. 2(47), 2(45), 39, 2(7).
- **Verification status:** Verified against secondary reports only.
- **Limitations:** Verified against secondary reports (legal news / case summaries) and official-record search excerpts; official URL located but full judgment text not reviewed in this pass. One secondary source (lawtext.in) misreports the outcome; LiveLaw and Bar & Bench agree with the summary used here.
- **Dataset reference_sections:** CPA_2_47, CPA_2_45, CPA_39, CPA_2_7
